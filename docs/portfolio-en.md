# Herberth Cunha — Engineering Portfolio

## Business first. Technology with purpose.

I am a Software Engineer and Cloud, Data & AI Architect focused on turning real operational problems into reliable systems. I work from business context and domain rules through architecture, delivery and production operation.

My role is not limited to implementing a platform. I map the decision the business needs to make, the people and processes involved, the source of truth, the cost of failure and the indicators that prove whether the solution is working. Technology is selected after those constraints are understood.

## Cloud and Terraform focus

I use Terraform to make infrastructure decisions explicit and reviewable. My practical scope includes network and security boundaries, EC2/ARM64 workloads, S3, CloudFront, Lambda, DynamoDB, RDS/PostgreSQL, SQS/DLQ, IAM, CloudWatch, DNS, budgets and delivery pipelines.

I design cloud systems around constraints rather than trends: expected load, cost ceiling, team capacity, security requirements, failure modes and the business consequence of downtime. Observability and FinOps are part of the initial architecture, not post-launch additions.

## An anonymized business case

In one operational intelligence initiative, the main challenge was not writing software. It was connecting field execution, evidence, commercial data and legacy systems into a trustworthy decision flow.

The work started by mapping actors, events, responsibilities, master data and operational indicators. That model then shaped a Flutter field application, a Python backend, a management web interface, relational persistence, integration adapters and automated tests around critical business flows.

The key outcome was architectural alignment with the operation: the system was designed to improve traceability, shorten the management feedback loop and create a reliable foundation for analytics and AI—without using AI where deterministic, explainable rules were the better choice.

## Engineering principles

- understand the business before choosing the stack;
- keep domain rules independent from delivery mechanisms;
- make critical decisions explainable and testable;
- design failure, rollback, security and cost upfront;
- use asynchronous processing only where eventual consistency is acceptable;
- operate with structured signals, health checks, alarms and runbooks;
- treat AI as a measurable capability, not a label.
